How we work

Argue on paper, not on site

The most expensive changes in any project are the ones decided after something exists. A client who could not read a plan sees the room for the first time when it is framed, and the conversation that should have happened in week one happens in week nine instead.

Visualisation moves that conversation earlier, which is its entire economic justification. Scaled plans do the same job for clearances — a door that will not open fully is obvious on paper and invisible in a description.

What visualisation must not do is overpromise. A rendering is illustrative: light, sheen and texture will differ from the built result, and material samples belong beside the image. Equally, these are not construction documents and cannot go to a permit office — that work belongs to a licensed architect or engineer, and saying so plainly is part of doing this honestly.
